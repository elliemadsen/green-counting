How do action words pair with topic words?

This site reads 347 syllabi sentence by sentence and groups their verbs and keywords into categories. The heatmap shows which kinds of doing are associated with which kinds of topics. Hover over a cell to see the courses that use that pairing most; click a cell to see how the pairing has changed over time; click any course to see its full profile.

Implementation Details

Every syllabus was split into sentences and the quantity of words in each verb and keyword category were counted. A sentence counts toward a verb group when it uses one of that group's verbs, and toward a keyword group when it mentions one of that group's topic words. Words are lemmatized to match their dictionary base form, and a word only counts as a keyword when it isn't being used as a verb in that sentence — so "building a wall" counts as a verb, while "the building" counts as a keyword. "Distinctiveness of Association" compares how often two word groups actually share a sentence with how often they would if the pairing were independent — a methodology known as Pointwise Mutual Information. The baseline is computed only over sentences that contain at least one categorized word, so administrative boilerplate (dates, grading policies, schedules) doesn't affect it.
