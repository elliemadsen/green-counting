How action words pair with topic words in architecture syllabi

This site reads 347 architecture course syllabi from 2020–2026, sentence by sentence, and grouped their verbs (what the course asks students to do) and their keywords (what the course talks about) into categories. This chart shows which kinds of doing go with which kinds of topics. Hover over a cell to see the courses that use that pairing most; click a cell to see how the pairing has changed over time; click any course to see its full profile.

How this was made: every syllabus was split into sentences. A sentence counts toward a verb group when it uses one of that group's verbs, and toward a keyword group when it mentions one of that group's topic words (words are matched by their dictionary base form, and a word only counts as a keyword when it isn't being used as a verb in that sentence — so "building a wall" counts as a verb, while "the building" counts as a keyword). "Distinctiveness of Association" compares how often two groups actually share a sentence with how often they would if the pairing were pure coincidence — a technical measure known as Pointwise Mutual Information. The coincidence baseline is computed only over sentences that contain at least one categorized word, so administrative boilerplate (dates, grading policies, schedules) doesn't distort it.
